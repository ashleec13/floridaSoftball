About the Gators

The Florida Gators softball team represents the University of Florida and is one of the most successful programs in NCAA Division I softball. Led for many years by head coach Tim Walton, the Gators have built a reputation as a consistent national powerhouse.

Florida’s key achievements include two NCAA national championships (2014 and 2015), where they became back-to-back champions and established themselves among the elite programs in college softball. The team has also made multiple appearances in the Women’s College World Series (WCWS), reaching the finals several times, including strong runs in 2009, 2011, and 2017.

In addition to national success, the Gators have dominated the SEC (Southeastern Conference) with multiple SEC regular season and tournament titles, consistently finishing near the top of one of the toughest conferences in college softball.

Sunflower Saturday

“Sunflower Saturday” is one of the most meaningful traditions in Florida Gators softball. The tradition began in 2009 when the team met Heather Braswell, a young girl battling brain cancer through the Friends of Jaclyn Foundation. Heather quickly became an honorary member of the team, attending practices, games, and spending time in the dugout with the players. Sunflowers were her favorite flower, so the team began wearing them in their hair to honor and support her.

After Heather sadly passed away in 2014, the Gators continued the tradition to keep her memory alive and raise awareness for pediatric cancer. During Sunflower Sunday, players wear bright yellow sunflowers, yellow accessories, and sometimes special uniforms to represent strength, hope, and support for children fighting cancer. The event has grown into a symbol of unity and compassion within Florida softball and remains one of the program’s most respected traditions.
